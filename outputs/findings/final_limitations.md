# TechScape: Final Academic Limitations & Epistemic Boundaries

## 1. Scope of Empirical Claims

All empirical conclusions produced by the TechScape platform describe observed characteristics of **online job vacancy advertisements in Sri Lanka collected in August 2026 ($n = 80$)** and official macroeconomic publications (Department of Census & Statistics, Central Bank of Sri Lanka).

They must NOT be cited as:
1. An exhaustive census of all employed IT professionals in Sri Lanka.
2. A deterministic forecasting or predictive machine learning model.
3. Guaranteed individual employment or salary outcomes.

---

## 2. Specific Academic Boundaries

### 2.1. Cross-Sectional Sample Scope ($n = 80$)
- Sourced across 39 prominent tech enterprises.
- Reflects active recruitment flow through formalized digital job boards (`TopJobs.lk`, `LinkedIn Sri Lanka`, `ITPro.lk`).
- Informal hiring, direct campus placements, and private referrals are unobserved.

### 2.2. Salary Non-Disclosure Selection Bias
- **56.25% of postings** omitted numerical compensation figures.
- Organizations disclosing numerical compensation tend to be either structured entry-level programs or export software companies advertising competitive USD-pegged structures.
- Salary percentiles represent the disclosing subset ($n = 35$) without artificial zero-imputation.

### 2.3. Job Advertisement Demand (Flow) vs. Employment Stock
- Vacancies measure marginal hiring demand at the collection window.
- The volume of job openings for a skill indicates employer hiring velocity, not the total size of the existing codebase or total employed workforce.

### 2.4. Skill Keyword Stacking
- Online job advertisements frequently enumerate wishlist technologies or broad toolsets that may exceed daily operational prerequisites.

### 2.5. Macroeconomic Distinction
- National unemployment statistics (DCS LFS) span all economic sectors across the island.
- The IT-BPM sector represents a specialized high-value knowledge export industry (~160,000 professionals); macro youth unemployment trends (12.8%) provide national context but cannot be causally linked to IT hiring swings.
